Our sea salt from France was tested for lead, arsenic, cadmium and mercury. All four results were below the limits listed on its certificate of analysis. The results report trace amounts, and we are sharing the exact numbers and full certificate below.

Our French sea salt contains sodium, magnesium, potassium and calcium. These minerals play roles in fluid balance, nerve signals and muscle function. Sea salt is mostly sodium chloride. The full mineral analysis appears in the certificate below.

Why sea salt is tested

These elements can occur naturally in soil and water. Human activity can also add to their levels. They can enter food ingredients from the environment. The FDA explains how these metals can get into food.

What the numbers mean

PPM means parts per million. For a solid ingredient, 1 ppm equals 1 microgram per gram. The lead result of 0.57 ppm means 0.57 micrograms of lead per gram of the salt described in this certificate.

Less than 0.01 ppm means the amount was reported as below that level. It does not mean zero.

Common questions

Did the salt meet the listed heavy metal limits

Yes. All four reported heavy metal results are below the limits printed on this certificate.

How small are the reported amounts

The test found very small amounts of lead and arsenic in the sea salt. The numbers in the table show how much was found. Cadmium and mercury were reported as less than the amounts shown. All four reported amounts were below one part per million by weight.

For scale, 0.57 ppm lead equals 0.000057% of the salt’s weight. The arsenic result of 0.06 ppm equals 0.000006%. These are very small fractions by weight. Trace amounts means small amounts are present or may be present, rather than a result of zero.

Trace metals in everyday foods

Trace metals can be found in everyday foods, including rice, spinach, other vegetables and seafood.
